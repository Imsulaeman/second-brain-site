# Viktor Frankl

Austrian psychiatrist, neurologist, and Holocaust survivor. Founder of [[concepts/logotherapy|logotherapy]] — the Third Viennese School of Psychotherapy, after Freud's psychoanalysis and Adler's individual psychology.

## Life

Born 1905 in Vienna. Studied psychiatry and neurology; ran the suicide prevention ward at the Am Steinhof psychiatric hospital for four years, where he assessed and treated approximately twelve thousand severely depressed patients. Before the war, he had already developed the foundations of logotherapy.

In 1942, he was deported to Theresienstadt. He subsequently survived Auschwitz, Kaufering, and Türkheim (a satellite camp of Dachau). His wife, mother, father, and brother all died in the camps. His manuscript for *The Doctor and the Soul* was confiscated at Auschwitz.

He was liberated in 1945. Within days of returning to Vienna, he dictated *Man's Search for Meaning* in nine days from memory.

He died in 1997.

## Intellectual Position

Frankl's central claim: the primary human drive is not pleasure (Freud) or power (Adler) but [[concepts/will-to-meaning|the will to meaning]]. This drive can be frustrated — producing [[concepts/existential-vacuum|existential vacuum]] — or fulfilled. Frustration is not automatically pathological; it is sometimes proof of one's humanness.

Where Freud looked backward (childhood, unconscious drives) and Adler looked inward (inferiority, compensation), Frankl looked forward: toward the meaning the person still has left to fulfill. Logotherapy is prospective, not retrospective.

He also critiqued pan-determinism — the assumption that man is fully conditioned by biological, psychological, or sociological forces. His counterclaim, sharpened by direct experience in the camps: even in conditions of total external control, [[concepts/last-freedom|the last freedom]] — choosing one's attitude — cannot be taken.

## Key Contributions

- [[concepts/logotherapy]] — meaning-centered psychotherapy
- [[concepts/will-to-meaning]] — primary motivational force in human life
- [[concepts/existential-vacuum]] — the epidemic emptiness of modernity
- [[concepts/last-freedom]] — attitude choice as irreducible human capacity

## Key Quote

> "Everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the last of the human freedoms — to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances, to choose one's own way."

Yanis Varoufakis is a Greek economist, academic, and former finance minister who gained international prominence during the 2015 Greek debt crisis. He is known for his sharp critiques of mainstream economics, the Eurozone's handling of sovereign debt, and the structural flaws of contemporary capitalism.

In *Talking to My Daughter About the Economy* (2018), Varoufakis presents a deliberately non-technical history of capitalism aimed at young readers. The book uses personal stories, literary references (Faust, Frankenstein, The Matrix), and historical episodes (POW camp markets, Aboriginal dispossession) to explain how market society emerged, why debt and profit are inseparable, how banks create money, and why unemployment and crises are systemic rather than accidental.

His core position across the book is that the economy is not a natural or neutral system but a set of human-made rules that can and should be questioned and redesigned. He consistently returns to the idea that inequality, recurring crises, and the power of finance are features of the current rules rather than bugs.

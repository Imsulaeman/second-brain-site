# Toyota

Japanese automaker founded in 1937. Originated the Toyota Production System (TPS) — the operational philosophy that became the global "lean manufacturing" template. The TPS culture produced multiple thinking tools that escaped the factory floor and became standard across product, design, and software disciplines: [[concepts/5-whys]], [[concepts/kaizen|kaizen]], andon, jidoka, just-in-time.

## Origin

Toyota grew out of Toyoda Automatic Loom Works, founded by [[entities/sakichi-toyoda]]. Sakichi's son Kiichiro pivoted the family business into automobiles in the 1930s. The Toyoda → Toyota rename came in 1937 (the Japanese characters for "Toyota" take 8 strokes — considered lucky — and the new spelling severed the company from a single family name).

## Why Toyota Matters Outside Cars

The reason Toyota appears all over decision-science, UX, and software literature isn't the cars — it's that Toyota turned **manufacturing diagnosis into a culture of root-cause thinking**. Three exports:

- **5 Whys** — iterative root-cause drill ([[concepts/5-whys]])
- **Kaizen** — continuous incremental improvement as everyone's job ([[concepts/kaizen]])
- **Andon / stop-the-line authority** — any worker can halt production to flag a defect; the system treats stopping as cheaper than shipping a flawed unit

These are organizational design choices, not just techniques. They presume the people closest to the work see causes the executives don't, and they build the procedural rights to act on what they see.

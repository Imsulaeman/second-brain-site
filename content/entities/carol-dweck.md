# Carol Dweck

Carol Dweck is a psychologist best known for the distinction between fixed and [[concepts/growth-mindset|growth mindsets]]: the belief that ability is either a stable trait or something that can be developed through effort, strategy, and feedback. In this wiki, she matters because she gives a psychological layer beneath older learning pages on [[concepts/deliberate-practice|deliberate practice]], [[concepts/plateau-of-latent-potential|delayed improvement]], and continuous improvement.

Her core contribution in *[[sources/the-power-of-believing-that-you-can-improve]]* is not merely motivational. She reframes what difficulty means. Under a fixed mindset, difficulty feels like exposure. Under a growth mindset, difficulty becomes information about where learning is happening. That shift changes how students respond to error, challenge, praise, and failure.

Dweck also sharpens a recurring distinction already present elsewhere in the wiki: outcomes versus process. Her argument against praising raw intelligence parallels other ideas here that favor systems, repetition, feedback loops, and compounding over one-off performances or identity verdicts.

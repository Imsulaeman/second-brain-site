# Greg Brockman

Greg Brockman is co-founder and (former) President of OpenAI. Before OpenAI he was CTO of Stripe, which he joined as employee #3 in 2010 and helped scale from early startup to infrastructure for much of the internet's commerce. He dropped out of Harvard to join Stripe. At OpenAI he co-founded the organization with Sam Altman, Ilya Sutskever, and others in 2015, serving as CTO before transitioning to President. He took a sabbatical in 2024.

His two ingested essays are unusually candid reflections on what he actually did in both roles — not what a CTO is supposed to do, but what he discovered the job to be through trial and error.

---

## The CTO discovery process

At Stripe, Brockman found himself unable to do all parts of the CTO job well. His response was systematic: he surveyed ~20 CTOs, hired a VP Engineering to cover his weak areas, and mapped which activities drained vs restored him. His key finding: most CTOs are facilitators, not chief architects. The "technical visionary" model is rare; most CTOs spend their time unblocking people, managing information flow, and making organizational decisions. See [[concepts/role-discovery]].

The feedback loop problem: once you delegate deeply enough, you lose honest ground-level signal. Brockman's fix was returning to coding — not always the highest-leverage use of time, but the activity that recharged him and gave him direct feedback about what was actually happening in the codebase. See [[concepts/energy-management]].

At OpenAI, a different pattern: find a problem bigger than any role (beneficial AI → find the right people → discover your unique contribution from what the org actually needs). His contribution was identifying engineering as the research bottleneck — projects like Gym and Universe existed because of this diagnosis. See [[concepts/role-discovery]].

# Vinh Giang

Australian communication coach and former magician; founder of the training company Stage. Giang is the third of three languages in his own household — Teochew, then Vietnamese, then English — and grew up teased for his accent to the point of refusing to speak in class. He traces his communication career to a teenage eBay scheme: at 13, using his parents' credit card during a four-week statement gap, he bought MP3 players in bulk and resold them to classmates for $3,000 in a week, discovering he'd earned 30x his cherry-farm wage. The lesson he took from getting caught wasn't about the money — it was that the limits he'd assumed were real (how much money is possible, what he was "born" capable of) turned out to be negotiable.

He spent years living in Southern California, including downtown LA, specifically to study storytelling from Hollywood producers like Brian Grazer. His stage persona leans on his magician background — treating the voice as a playable instrument and treating standing ovations as a technique (locking eyes with the front row during a bow) rather than a spontaneous audience reaction.

In [[sources/art-of-conversation|Art of Conversation]], a masterclass transcript, Giang teaches the [[concepts/para-framework|PARA framework]] and [[concepts/three-two-one-framework|3-2-1 framework]] for structuring spoken contributions, the [[concepts/mastery-cycle|Mastery Cycle]] for skill development, and a version of the [[concepts/dunning-kruger-learning-curve|Dunning-Kruger learning curve]] (which he calls the Slope of Mastery) mapped specifically onto communication skill. His central claim is that voice, accent, and rambling are learned behaviors — usually absorbed unconsciously from admired role models in childhood — not fixed traits, and are therefore just as changeable as any other habit.

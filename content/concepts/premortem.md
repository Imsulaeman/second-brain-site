A premortem is Gary Klein's procedure, endorsed by Kahneman: before committing to a plan, imagine it failed catastrophically, then write the history of how that failure happened. Legitimizes dissent and surfaces risks the group's optimism suppressed.

## Why it works

Planning meetings reward confidence; premortem gives cover to skeptics. It fights [[concepts/planning-fallacy]], [[concepts/wysiati]], and organizational lethargy when bad reference-class data is noted then ignored.

Complements [[concepts/inside-view-outside-view|reference class forecasting]] and checklists — organizations think more slowly and can impose orderly procedures humans resist individually.

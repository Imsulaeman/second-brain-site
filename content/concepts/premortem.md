# Premortem

Also called **prospective hindsight** — psychologist [[entities/Gary-Klein|Gary Klein]]'s procedure, endorsed by [[entities/Daniel-Kahneman|Daniel Kahneman]] and extended to personal life by [[entities/Daniel-Levitin|Daniel Levitin]].

**Postmortem:** after a disaster, experts figure out what went wrong.

**Pre-mortem:** before committing, imagine the plan failed catastrophically, identify what could go wrong, then prevent those failures or minimize damage.

## Organizational Use (Kahneman)

Before a group commits to a plan, imagine it failed and write the history of how. Legitimizes dissent and surfaces risks optimism suppressed. Fights [[concepts/planning-fallacy]], [[concepts/wysiati]], and organizational lethargy when bad reference-class data gets noted then ignored.

Complements [[concepts/inside-view-outside-view|reference class forecasting]] and checklists — organizations can impose orderly procedures humans resist individually.

## Personal Use (Levitin)

Levitin's Montreal cascade — lockout → broken window → forgotten passport → missed seat — taught him that [[concepts/stress-impairs-judgment|stress impairs judgment]] in ways you can't detect in the moment. The pre-mortem response is systems installed while calm:

- [[concepts/designated-place-system|Designated places]] for keys, passport, glasses
- Travel backups (cloud photos of documents)
- Physical fail-safes (combination lock with spare key)
- Pre-written medical questions ([[concepts/number-needed-to-treat|NNT]], side effects, quality of life)

The principle: **don't manufacture the chain of reasoning on the spot** when cortisol is active. Pre-mortem the questions and systems before you need them.

Medical decisions are the stress-test case — but the frame applies to financial and social decisions too.

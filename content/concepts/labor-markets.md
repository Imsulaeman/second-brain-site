Labor markets are the arenas in which people sell their time and effort in exchange for wages. In market societies they are not neutral matching mechanisms but institutions shaped by power, law, and the prior separation of people from subsistence.

*Talking to My Daughter About the Economy* uses the story of Wasily (a PhD graduate unable to find work) to show that unemployment can persist even when people are willing to accept very low wages. The book argues that the "market" for labor often fails to clear because employers hold structural power and because the system does not automatically generate enough demand for the goods that additional workers would produce.

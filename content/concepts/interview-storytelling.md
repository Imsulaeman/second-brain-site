# Interview Storytelling

Using narrative from your experience rolodex to answer interview questions — the strategy [[entities/Dino-Martinez|Dino Martinez]] teaches once employers already like you on paper.

## Why Stories Beat Adjectives

Mock-interview trap: "Why hire you over four peers?" → "I'm dedicated, hardworking, good communicator." That implies the other four aren't. You haven't articulated what you uniquely bring.

Stories breathe life into the resume. They show professional maturity, conflict handling, learning from mistakes, and how experiences shaped development — not just that an event occurred.

## Rolodex of Experiences

You arrive with a lifetime of experiences. The task: select stories relevant to the position, scholarship, or school — and practice telling them. Interviewing is a skill set requiring practice (career centers, mock interviews, behavioral question banks).

## What Interviewers Actually Want

For behavioral prompts ("tell me about a time when you disagreed with your supervisor"), the conflict detail often matters less than:
- What you did
- What you learned
- How it shaped your development

Stop at "here's the mistake, nobody got hurt" and you've technically answered — but a story with insight is more compelling.

## Format Flexibility

Storytelling works across:
- Behavioral ("tell me about a time…")
- Hypotheticals (answer with a similar real episode)
- Technical/knowledge questions (client projects illustrating Java vs C++)

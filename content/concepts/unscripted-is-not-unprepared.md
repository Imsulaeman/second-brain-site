# Unscripted Is Not Unprepared

Giving an unscripted talk is not the same as winging it. The words are found in the moment — the structure, the steps, and the destination are prepared just as rigorously as they would be in a scripted talk.

The confusion comes from conflating two different things: the preparation and the delivery mode. A scripted talk prepares both the ideas and the exact words. An unscripted talk prepares the ideas, the sequence, and each waypoint — and trusts the speaker to find the words live.

The preparation for an unscripted talk is not less than for a scripted one. In some ways it demands more: the speaker must know the material deeply enough to articulate it clearly under the pressure of performance, without a safety net of memorized sentences.

## What Preparation Looks Like

- Know the [[concepts/throughline]] cold — the single idea the whole talk serves
- Map every step of the [[concepts/talk-as-journey]] — label each section with a short phrase
- Rehearse the transitions between steps — the hardest part of unscripted delivery is moving cleanly from one section to the next
- Know the subject matter well enough that no question about it will derail you mid-talk

## When Unscripted Works Best

- The talk is built around a single personal story (narrative is naturally sequenced)
- The speaker is a genuine domain expert (deep knowledge replaces the safety net)
- The speaking context is conversational rather than performative

When the talk involves complicated research, unfamiliar data, or precise wording that matters — scripted is safer.

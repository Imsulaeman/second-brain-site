Empathy is the choice to feel with another person.

It requires taking their perspective as their truth, staying out of judgment, recognising the emotion they are experiencing, and communicating that recognition. These four qualities come from nursing scholar [[entities/theresa-wiseman|Theresa Wiseman]] and were popularised by [[entities/brene-brown|Brené Brown]].

The image that makes the distinction clearest is the hole. When someone is at the bottom of a deep hole shouting for help, empathy climbs down and sits with them. [[concepts/sympathy|Sympathy]] stays at the top and offers comfort from a safe distance: "Ooh, that's bad... you want a sandwich?"

Because empathy demands feeling with, it is vulnerable. You must locate in yourself some version of the feeling the other person is carrying. Without that touch, the response easily slides into silver-lining language that begins with "at least." Those responses minimise the experience and break connection.

[[entities/jonathan-juravich|Jonathan Juravich]] points out that the root of empathy is awareness: a noticing of what is happening in and around you so that you can make a choice. Awareness of your own emotions comes first. Only then can you register the emotions of others and respond with action instead of abstraction.

Common metaphors like "walk in someone else's shoes" often fail with young children because they are taken literally. Specific, observable practices work better: repeated prompts that ask a child to name a moment of pride, frustration, excitement, or laughter from their day, followed by the adult doing the same. Over time the child begins reporting the feelings of friends as naturally as their own.

In design, empathy is the required first stage. The Empathize step in [[concepts/design-thinking|design thinking]] only produces useful insight when it reaches the depth of feeling with users rather than collecting data about them. Shallow empathy creates personas that never change decisions. Real empathy changes what gets built.

Empathy is not infinite. It is a limited resource that requires choice and energy. It also cannot be performed from a position of superiority. The moment judgment or "at least" enters, the connection is lost.

The same capacity that lets someone sit with another's pain is what lets designers, teachers, and parents move from noticing to useful action.

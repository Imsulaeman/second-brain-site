# Inflection Points

[[entities/Howard-Stevenson]] borrows the term from mathematics: an inflection point is where there is no tangent — no clear direction, no obvious path forward. In a life, inflection points are the moments that break the prevailing trajectory and open the possibility of a new one.

Examples from Stevenson's own life: his wife leaving (became a single parent). Giving up tenure in 1978 despite having just earned it. His cardiac arrest in 2009. A friend dying of a heart attack at 43 — "he was supposed to be my trustee."

## The Failure Mode

Most people pass through inflection points without recognizing them as such. The external event registers (a divorce, a layoff, a health scare), but the deeper question — *does this free me to change direction?* — never gets asked.

The default is to treat the inflection point as a problem to return to normal from, not as an opening. "Return to normal" is exactly the wrong frame when the normal was itself the constraint.

## The Gift Frame

Treating inflection points as gifts means two things:

1. **Pause and look.** The inflection itself is not the direction — it's the opening. You have to stop and ask: what do I want to do that I couldn't do before this happened?

2. **Both negative and positive inflection points work.** A divorce is an obvious opening. But getting tenure, landing a major promotion, finishing a book — positive events also break the prior trajectory. The question applies equally: what does this free me to do or become?

Stevenson pauses at inflection points even when they're negative ("things happen") and explicitly asks whether they open a door. If yes, he walks through. If he'd interviewed tenured professors and not found them happy, why would he walk down the same path?

## The Connection to Living Forward

Inflection points are also tests of [[concepts/live-life-forward]]. The risk at an inflection point is to spend it managing grief, anger, or regret — facing backward — rather than asking what comes next. The inflection is precisely the moment when the backward pull is strongest and the forward question is most valuable.

Banking in a capitalist economy is not mainly a storage service. It is a system for creating money, extending claims on the future, and keeping the whole process of payment and repayment moving.

Varoufakis calls banking black magic because banks do not wait for someone else to save before they lend. In practice they create new purchasing power when they issue credit. That makes them central to growth, but it also means they sit at the center of systemic fragility. If lending contracts, confidence collapses, or repayments fail, [[concepts/recycling|recycling]] breaks down and [[concepts/crises|crises]] spread quickly.

The book also treats banking as a political institution. A society that lets private banks create most money is making a political choice about who gets to allocate risk, who receives credit first, and who is rescued when the chain breaks.

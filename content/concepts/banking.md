Banking is the institutional mechanism that creates most of the money in a modern economy through lending and simultaneously recycles purchasing power so that goods and services can be bought and sold.

In *Talking to My Daughter About the Economy*, Varoufakis describes banking as "black magic" because banks do not merely intermediate existing savings — they create new money when they make loans. This money-creation function is essential for growth but also the source of instability: when lending stops or expectations collapse, the recycling of money breaks down and crises (such as the Great Depression or the Greek debt crisis) follow. The book emphasizes that this power is political, not technical, and has been repeatedly mismanaged when treated as a neutral technical process.

# Universal Basic Income

Universal basic income (UBI) is a fixed, unconditional cash payment to every member of society regardless of employment status or wealth. It is the only known policy design that fully eliminates the [[concepts/welfare-trap]].

The mechanism: because the payment is universal and unconditional, any wages earned supplement it rather than replace it. There is no income threshold at which benefits cut off. The cliff edge that creates the trap doesn't exist.

UBI has been proposed since the 18th century and has been championed by economists across the political spectrum. Small-scale experiments have been run in Finland, Kenya, Stockton CA, and elsewhere. Results consistently show recipients spend money on food, education, and health rather than alcohol or idleness — the most common objection.

## Why It Remains Hypothetical

Local experiments don't answer the national-scale questions: what happens to labor supply across an entire economy? How is it funded? What happens to inflation? Does it replace existing programs or supplement them?

These aren't reasons to reject UBI — they're reasons it's hard to test. The evidence from pilots is promising but limited by scope.

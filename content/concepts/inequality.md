Inequality in market societies is not primarily the result of differences in talent, effort, or intelligence, but of the historical process that separated most people from direct access to the means of subsistence.

*Talking to My Daughter About the Economy* opens with this question and answers it by contrasting the situation of Aboriginal Australians with the emergence of market society in Europe. Once people must sell their labor to survive, the owners of capital gain a structural advantage that compounds over time. The book treats inequality as a designed feature of the rules rather than an unfortunate side effect that growth will eventually solve.

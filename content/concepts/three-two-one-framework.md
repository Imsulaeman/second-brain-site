# 3-2-1 Framework

A tool for answering a question on the spot — a job interview's "tell us about yourself," a Q&A prompt, any moment that demands speech without preparation time. [[entities/Vinh-Giang|Vinh Giang]] built it as the antidote to the specific panic of an open-ended topic: asked to talk about "business" or "travel" or "yourself" with no constraint, the brain tries to survey every possible angle at once and produces nothing coherent.

## The Mechanism

3-2-1 works by pre-committing to one of three sentence starters before speaking:

- **Three** — "There are three steps to X" (or three types, three reasons)
- **Two** — "There are two types of X"
- **One** — "The one thing about X is..."

Naming the format first turns an unbounded topic into a bounded one. The sentence starter forces the brain to search for exactly one, two, or three things instead of everything — the same effect PARA's four beats have on a group contribution, applied to a single answer under time pressure.

Giang demonstrates the "one thing" starter is the easiest of the three and the one people default to under pressure; "three steps" produces the most content and, he notes, is structurally identical to a listicle — the same format YouTube educational content and how-to articles use, because it's a naturally good vehicle for teaching a sequence.

## Worked Example: The Interview Opener

Applied to "tell us a little about yourself," a formless answer tends to spiral through hobbies, family, and work in no particular order until the interviewer's attention drifts. The one-thing version instead commits early: "The one thing about me I'd love to share is that I loved magic as a kid — because to me a magic trick is just a problem you can't solve, and I've gotten very good at problem-solving," bridging directly into why that trait matters for the job being interviewed for.

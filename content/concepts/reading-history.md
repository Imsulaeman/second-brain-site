# Reading History

Reading history means reading narrative accounts of the past while remembering that historical facts reach you already selected, arranged, and interpreted.

Adler emphasizes that history is not raw fact but written account. This means the reader has to judge not only what happened, but what the historian is doing with evidence, causation, scope, and significance. The practical payoff is large: history often shapes action by expanding the reader's sense of what has happened before, what is possible, and what may be avoided.

The concept connects naturally to [[concepts/critical-thinking|critical thinking]] and [[concepts/correlation-vs-causality|correlation vs causality]], because historical writing constantly invites judgments about explanation, relevance, and causal story.

# PARA Framework

A four-beat structure — Point, Action, Result, Ask — for entering a live conversation with a distilled contribution instead of a scattered one. [[entities/Vinh-Giang|Vinh Giang]] built it around a specific failure mode: someone wants to join a group discussion, but without a structure the brain tries to say everything about the topic at once, the contribution comes out incoherent, and the group moves on without them.

## The Four Beats

**Point** — state the single claim you want to make. Not a topic, a claim: "I never realized the importance of rest when it comes to high performance."

**Action** — name a concrete thing you did as a result of that point. "I've stopped working after 5pm and started sleeping by 9pm."

**Result** — report what the action produced, including something unexpected. "My quality of work improved. So did my quality of life."

**Ask** — hand the conversation back to the group with a question. "How do you all incorporate rest as high performers?"

The Ask beat is doing two jobs at once: it closes the contribution cleanly (so the speaker doesn't ramble into a second point) and it hands control of the floor back to the group, avoiding the failure mode of being the person who monopolizes the conversation.

## Worked Example: Meetings and Visibility

Giang argues PARA is not just a social-conversation tool — it's a career tool, because it's the mechanism for converting silence into [[concepts/workplace-visibility|visibility]] at work. His example: in a team meeting about automations, instead of staying quiet, contribute with **Point** ("we need to leverage more automation"), **Action** ("I started using a scheduling app two weeks ago"), **Result** ("I saved 10 hours; scaled across the team that's 500 hours"), **Ask** ("would it be useful for me to train the team on this?"). The structure converts a vague desire to contribute into a specific, credible pitch — and specific, credible pitches are what get noticed for promotion.

# Void Inventory

A breakup doesn't end a relationship — it ends several things simultaneously. The void inventory is the practice of identifying every distinct loss, not just the romantic one, and addressing each separately.

A relationship typically embeds: a social life built around the partner, shared routines, mutual friends, a community (church, gym, neighborhood), an identity as part of a couple, physical spaces that held the relationship. When the relationship ends, all of these end with it.

Most people treat heartbreak as a single wound. It is usually five or six. Recovery that only addresses the romantic dimension stalls because the other voids remain open and continue to generate pain.

## The Inventory

The categories to audit:
- **Social life** — who did you spend time with through them? Where do those people go now?
- **Identity** — how did you define yourself in the relationship? What's the replacement?
- **Activities** — what did you do together that you no longer do?
- **Community** — church, gym, neighborhood groups, couples' friendships
- **Physical space** — empty walls, unused chairs, places you no longer go

Each one that remains open is a source of ongoing pain. Each one that gets addressed closes a wound.

## Why It Works

The void inventory reframes recovery as a project with enumerable components rather than a single emotional state to wait out. Each closed void is measurable progress. The most effective recoveries are active — identifying the specific gaps and filling them deliberately — not passive.

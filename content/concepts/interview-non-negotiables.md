# Interview Non-Negotiables

The non-negotiable conditions you need — inside and outside work — to be happy in a role. [[entities/Ashley-Rizzotto|Ashley Rizzotto]]'s first strategy for mastering interviews: know these before you walk in, so you can advocate instead of performing.

## How to Find Them

Tune into activities and surroundings that bring joy:

- Do you need collaboration or solo work?
- City hustle or rural quiet?
- What does well-being require beyond the job description?

Rizzotto's list grew after a Brooklyn pizza lover accepted a small North Carolina college town — hour-long drives for mediocre crust, slower pace, no public transit. She realized priorities must include what makes you happy **outside** work too.

Your non-negotiable list should include what wakes you up excited for work and what you look forward to when you leave.

## Connect to Motivation

Non-negotiables link [[concepts/career-strengths-identification|strengths]] to meaningful experience. As you uncover each strength, add it to the non-negotiable list. You must learn to listen to what motivates you and ask for it in the interview.

## In the Interview

When research reveals a gap between your list and the posting, advocate: make the interviewer know how important that element is to your success. Rizzotto advocated for independence, creativity, and a coworker "family" — and heard "career family" echoed back in the offer.

The opposite failure mode: telling them what they want to hear (great at criticism, loves logistics) while hiding what you actually need.

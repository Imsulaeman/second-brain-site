# Seven Deadly Sins of Speaking

[[entities/Julian-Treasure|Julian Treasure]]'s label for seven common habits that make speech hard to listen to. Not a complete taxonomy of bad communication — but large habits most people fall into at some point.

The sins are structural: each one gives the listener a reason to withdraw attention before the speaker's actual point arrives.

## The Seven

1. **Gossip** — speaking ill of someone who isn't present. Listeners know the pattern: today's gossiper is tomorrow's subject.

2. **Judging** — evaluating the listener while talking to them. Hard to stay open when you sense you're being weighed and found wanting.

3. **Negativity** — chronic pessimism that makes every topic feel heavy. Treasure's example: told it's October 1, his mother replied, "I know, isn't it dreadful?"

4. **Complaining** — Treasure calls this Britain's "national sport." Complaining spreads viral misery rather than lightness.

5. **Excuses** — the blamethrower who passes responsibility outward. Listeners disengage from people who won't own their actions.

6. **Embroidery / exaggeration** — inflating language until real awe has no word left ("awesome" on everything). The slope ends at lying, and people don't listen to known liars.

7. **Dogmatism** — stating opinions as if they were facts. When fact and opinion collapse, the listener is bombarded with assertions they can't verify.

## Why It Matters

These habits operate on the relationship layer, not the content layer. A speaker can have a valid argument and still lose the room through gossip, judgment, or dogmatism. The positive counterweight is [[concepts/hail-framework|HAIL]] — honesty, authenticity, integrity, and love as foundations for speech people want to receive.

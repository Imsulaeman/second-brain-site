# Twenty-Word Introduction

A [[concepts/about-you-introduction|listener-oriented]] self-introduction capped at roughly 20 words — designed as a sound bite that earns "Tell me more," not a compressed résumé.

[[entities/Rebecca-Okamoto|Rebecca Okamoto]] built this after studying first-impression science and viral headline patterns. The constraint forces clarity: multitasking audiences won't absorb a credential dump, but they will lean in when one sentence promises a benefit they care about.

## Design Principles

- **Think sound bite** — grab attention; don't explain everything
- **About you, not about me** — lead with what you do for *them*
- **Trigger curiosity** — success means hearing "Tell me more"

## Five Formulas

### 1. Benefit (career-focused)

> I help [target audience] achieve [a benefit they desire]

Example: *"I help worried job hunters confidently explain why they're the ideal candidate to hire."*

The benefit answers: *How will my life be different?*

### 2. Breakthrough (differentiation)

> I help [target audience] achieve [benefit] **without** [negative consequence]

Example: *"I help established brands in competitive markets rapidly reach new audiences without increasing marketing spend."*

The word **without** carries uniqueness — it names the pain you remove.

### 3. Passion (mission-driven, pivoting, student)

> I'm passionate about [something I value] to achieve [something my target audience values]

Example: *"I'm passionate about helping people in need and creating opportunities that change lives."*

Use when career history is thin but purpose is clear.

### 4. Strength

> I'm known for [my strength] to achieve [something my target audience values]

Example: *"I'm known for my critical thinking and turning information into actionable insights."*

### 5. Mission

> I'm on a mission to [ABC] to achieve [something my target audience values]

Example: *"I'm on a mission to bridge the health care divide and make a lasting difference for vulnerable communities."*

## Audience Variation

Different audiences value different benefits. Keep multiple versions — what works for one listener may miss another. The formulas are templates, not a single permanent tagline.

**Definition**: Reasoning from specific observations, examples, or patterns to a general conclusion. The conclusion is *probable* but not guaranteed.

**Core Structure**
1. Observe multiple examples
2. Notice a pattern or trend
3. Form a general conjecture or prediction
4. Use it to anticipate future cases

**Key Characteristics**
- Starts with data/experience
- Produces conjectures and forecasts
- Strength depends on quantity and quality of evidence
- Can be disproven by new evidence
- Common in science, business, medicine, history, and daily prediction

**Strengths**
- Enables learning from experience
- Useful when complete information is unavailable
- Supports theory formation and hypothesis generation

**Weaknesses**
- Risk of hasty generalization
- Pattern may break
- Cannot deliver certainty

**Relation to Deductive Reasoning**
Inductive reasoning generates the general rules and patterns that deductive reasoning later applies. See [[Deductive Reasoning]].

# Alignment Through Research

Matching internal needs ([[concepts/interview-non-negotiables|non-negotiables]] + [[concepts/career-strengths-identification|strengths]]) to external opportunities through deliberate information gathering before and during the job search.

[[entities/Ashley-Rizzotto|Ashley Rizzotto]]'s third interview strategy. Alignment is drawing dotted lines between your list and the job posting in front of you.

## Research Methods

- Gather information online about target roles
- Talk to professionals doing exactly what you want to do
- Network
- Test new responsibilities you might want on your resume

Once you sense what's out there and what you have internally, compare explicitly.

## Two Outcomes

**Gap found, role still worth pursuing:** prepare to advocate in the interview — make sure interviewers know how important the missing element is to your success.

**Gap found, role not worth pursuing:** step back. Don't settle for anything less than what you need to be happy.

## Alignment Signals

Strong alignment shows up when employers mirror your language. Rizzotto's offer: *"We already see you as part of our career family"* — echoing her non-negotiable about coworker family culture. That mirroring suggested the role would value her non-negotiables and utilize her strengths.

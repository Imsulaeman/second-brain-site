# HAIL Framework

Four foundations [[entities/Julian-Treasure|Julian Treasure]] says powerful speech should stand on. The acronym spells **HAIL** — to greet or acclaim enthusiastically — which is how Treasure thinks words will be received when these four are present.

## The Four Pillars

**Honesty** — being true in what you say; straight and clear. Not cruelty dressed as candor, but no hidden agenda in the words themselves.

**Authenticity** — being yourself rather than performing a persona. Treasure quotes a friend: "standing in your own truth."

**Integrity** — being your word. Doing what you say you'll do; being someone others can trust over time.

**Love** — wishing people well. Not romantic love. Two functions:
- It tempers honesty. "You look ugly this morning" may be honest but isn't necessary.
- It makes simultaneous judgment nearly impossible. Treasure isn't sure you can genuinely wish someone well and judge them at the same time.

## Relation to Empathy

The love pillar overlaps with [[concepts/empathy|empathy]]'s orientation — feeling *with* rather than evaluating from above — but HAIL is a speaker-side ethic, not a listener's protocol. It answers: *what stance should I take before I open my mouth?*

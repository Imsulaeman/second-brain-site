# Career Strengths Identification

Knowing what you're genuinely good at — the foundation for asking for what you need in [[concepts/interview-non-negotiables|non-negotiables]] and redirecting interviews toward value you actually deliver.

[[entities/Ashley-Rizzotto|Ashley Rizzotto]]'s second interview strategy.

## Discovery Methods

- **Compliments that stuck** — Rizzotto was told "you always have a story to tell"; she found strength in communication and pursued educating and empowering others
- **Best-friend description** — how someone who knows you would describe you to a stranger
- **Experience audits** — volunteer work, side projects, new responsibilities; note what you loved vs. what was missing

## Idealized vs. Actual Strengths

A critical distinction. Rizzotto's unpleasant job forced her to separate strengths she *claimed* in the interview (logistics, planning) from strengths she *actually* enjoyed using. Several idealized strengths masked a large share of work she hated.

Honest identification prevents accepting roles where your real strengths are a small fraction of responsibilities.

## Using Strengths in Interviews

When asked about a skill you have but don't want to center, acknowledge it — then redirect to strengths. Rizzotto's logistics answer: skilled, but redirected to how she brings value through communication and empowerment.

Pair with honest answers on weaknesses (criticism handling) rather than performing perfection.

[[entities/Dino-Martinez|Dino Martinez]] calls the same audit **research yourself**: before "why hire you over four peers," know what you bring beyond generic adjectives. Identified strengths become the [[concepts/interview-storytelling|stories]] you select from your experience rolodex.

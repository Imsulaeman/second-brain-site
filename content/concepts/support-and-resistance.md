Support and resistance are price levels where buying or selling has repeatedly turned the market. **Support** is where fresh buying previously stopped a decline; **resistance** is where rallies have failed to push higher. They are memory traces of past auction outcomes, not magic lines — but they matter because traders watch the same levels.

## How traders use them

In a range-bound stock, buy near support and sell near resistance can work for months. A **breakout** above resistance (or below support) often signals continuation — e.g. failure to clear $50 repeatedly, then a close above, invites a move to higher ground.

Pin bars and other reversal patterns gain strength at prior support/resistance, pivot points, weekly highs/lows, or Fibonacci levels — see [[concepts/trading-technical-indicator]].

## Limits

Levels shift as fundamentals change; false breakouts trap traders who treat lines as certainty. Combine with trend context ([[concepts/golden-cross-death-cross]], moving averages) and [[concepts/risk-reward-ratio|risk/reward]] on entries — not line touches alone.

Livermore's "line of least resistance" ([[concepts/line-of-least-resistance]]) is the macro cousin: wait for the market to show which side of the range won before committing size.

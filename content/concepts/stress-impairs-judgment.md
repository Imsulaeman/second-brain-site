# Stress Impairs Judgment

You're not at your best when you're stressed — and the dangerous part is you often can't tell.

[[entities/Daniel-Levitin|Daniel Levitin]]'s framing: the brain releases **cortisol** in threatening situations. Cortisol raises heart rate, modulates adrenaline, and clouds rational, logical thinking. Evolution built this for predator encounters — digestion, libido, and immune function shut down so metabolism goes to immediate survival. Rational analysis is collateral damage.

## The Meta-Problem

Cloudy thinking hides from itself. Levitin woke after too little sleep, worrying about a broken window, a contractor call, freezing weather, and Europe meetings — cortisol already flooding his brain. He didn't know his thinking was impaired *because* his thinking was impaired. He only discovered he'd forgotten his passport at the airport check-in counter.

The lesson: don't rely on in-moment clarity when stakes are high. [[entities/Daniel-Kahneman|Kahneman]]'s research shows logical System 2 degrades under stress — the same mechanism behind interview halos, medical panic, and financial decisions made in a crash.

## The Fix Is Before, Not During

Put systems in place while calm:

- [[concepts/premortem|Pre-mortem]] — imagine failures and prevent them
- [[concepts/designated-place-system|Designated places]] — so essentials don't depend on stressed recall
- Pre-written questions — especially for medical decisions ([[concepts/number-needed-to-treat|NNT]], side effects, quality of life)

Willpower in the hot moment is the wrong tool. Structure in the cold moment is the right one.

# Implementation Intentions

An implementation intention is a pre-decision about when, where, and how you will act. The formula:

> "I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]."

The cues that trigger habits most reliably are time and location. Implementation intentions hard-code both in advance, so when the moment arrives the brain doesn't deliberate — it executes.

---

## The Evidence

In 2001, British researchers divided 248 people into three groups trying to build exercise habits:

- **Group 1 (control):** just tracked workouts
- **Group 2 (motivation):** tracked + read material on benefits of exercise
- **Group 3 (implementation intention):** same as Group 2, plus filled in: *"During the next week, I will partake in at least 20 minutes of vigorous exercise on [DAY] at [TIME] in [PLACE]."*

Results: 35–38% of Groups 1 and 2 exercised at least once per week. **91% of Group 3 did** — more than double. The motivational presentation given to Group 2 had no meaningful impact. The plan did.

The same pattern shows up across domains: flu shot scheduling, colonoscopy appointments, voter turnout ("what route are you taking to the polling station? what time are you going?"), tax compliance, stopping smoking. Hundreds of studies confirm: a specific plan doubles to triples follow-through rates.

---

## Clarity vs. Motivation

The usual diagnosis when a habit fails is a motivation problem. The actual problem is usually a clarity problem:

> "Many people think they lack motivation when what they really lack is clarity."

You intended to work out but never decided *when*. You wanted to read but the book was in the other room. You wanted to be productive but the morning started without a plan. Implementation intentions close the gap — not by increasing desire, but by removing ambiguity before it becomes a decision point.

Vague goals ("I want to eat healthier") leave room for rationalization. Specific plans ("I will eat a salad at 12:30 PM at my desk") do not.

## Applications

| Goal | Implementation Intention |
|---|---|
| Meditate | I will meditate for one minute at 7:00 AM in my kitchen. |
| Study | I will study Spanish for 20 minutes at 6:00 PM in my bedroom. |
| Exercise | I will exercise for one hour at 5:00 PM at my local gym. |
| Write | I will write 200 words at 9:00 PM in the living room. |

**Fresh start leverage:** People are more likely to follow through on a plan that starts on the first day of the week, month, or year. Hope is higher at natural transition points. If unsure when to start — start there.

**Focus leverage:** A specific plan also makes it easier to say no to competing demands. When your schedule is vague, small exceptions feel harmless. When the plan is explicit, deviations are visible.

## Habit Stacking as a Special Case

[[concepts/habit-stacking|Habit stacking]] replaces the time/location variable with an existing behavior:

> "After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]."

This is a special form of implementation intention where the trigger is a behavior you already do reliably — often more robust than a clock time, because the existing habit fires automatically regardless of schedule disruption.

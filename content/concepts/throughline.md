# Throughline

A throughline is the single clear idea that connects every part of a talk, essay, or narrative. Every section, story, and example either serves it or doesn't belong.

A talk without a throughline is a collection of related points. A talk with one is an argument. The distinction matters to the audience: with a throughline, each new section feels like a step forward; without one, it feels like a list.

The throughline is identified before writing begins, not discovered during it. The question is: what is the one thing I want the audience to leave with? Everything else gets evaluated against that answer. Material that doesn't serve the throughline gets cut, no matter how interesting it is on its own.

## As a Structural Test

The throughline functions as a filter during revision. For any section, ask: does this move the audience closer to the central idea, or does it digress? A strong section earns its place by being necessary, not just relevant.

This is harder than it sounds. Interesting tangents, useful context, and supporting evidence all feel like they belong. The throughline forces a choice: serve the idea or cut.

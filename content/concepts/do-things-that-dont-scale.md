# Do Things that Don't Scale

Startups do not usually take off by themselves. Founders have to make them take off with an early, laborious, unscalable push — the "crank" before the engine runs on its own.

## Recruit

Manual user acquisition is the classic unscalable activity. Stripe became famous inside YC for the "Collison installation": when someone said yes to the beta, they immediately set them up on their laptop.

Compound growth makes small early numbers powerful. 10% weekly growth turns 100 users into millions in a couple of years. You can start manual and transition later.

Airbnb's founders went door-to-door in New York (rolly bags, professional photos of listings). For a time that manual effort was the difference between life and death.

Early startups are fragile. Judging them by mature-company standards is a common mistake. The right question is how big the company could become if the founders do the right (sometimes seemingly inconsequential) things.

## Delight

You must also go to extraordinary lengths to make the first users happy.

Wufoo sent hand-written thank-you notes to every new user for as long as they could.

Engineers often resist customer hand-holding. Big companies set low expectations. Being small gives you the ability to deliver a level of service no big company can. "Insanely great" in the early stage often means the *experience* of being your user, not yet the polish of the product.

Trying too hard to delight initial users has never, in Graham's experience, lured a startup down a blind alley.

# Memorizing a Talk

Memorizing a talk is a reliable path to powerful delivery — but it contains a trap in the middle that stops most people short.

## The Three Stages

**Stage 1 — Early.** The speaker doesn't yet know the script but delivers from genuine knowledge and passion. Disorganized, but alive. The audience feels the energy.

**Stage 2 — The Robot Stage.** The speaker knows chunks but not the full arc. Gaps cause audible searching: "let me start that again," "just a minute." The talk sounds recited rather than meant. This is the danger zone because it feels like failure.

**Stage 3 — Fluency.** The words are so internalized that retrieving them requires no conscious attention. The speaker can think about meaning again. Passion returns — now with structure underneath.

The critical insight: **the robot stage is not a sign that memorization doesn't work. It is a predictable phase that passes only by continuing.** Most speakers who quit memorization quit at stage 2, then conclude the method failed them.

## Spoken Language vs. Written Language

Writing a script in prose and then reading or reciting it produces language that sounds unnatural when spoken aloud. Written language and spoken language are different registers.

The solution: generate the first draft by speaking, not writing. Dictate the talk into a recorder. Use the transcript as the raw material for the script. This preserves the rhythms and vocabulary of natural speech.

## Script, Notes, or Nothing

The choice isn't binary:
- **Full script, memorized** — highest ceiling, requires passing through the robot stage
- **Full script, read** — can work well if the speaker knows the material well enough to look up frequently and mean every sentence; Amanda Gorman's written language works because it's designed to be heard
- **Notes-guided** — a middle path; works when the speaker knows the material deeply and the talk follows one clear story
- **Unscripted** — sounds freshest; requires the clearest map of the journey, because improvisation without structure drifts

Unscripted is not the same as unprepared. The structure needs to be as solid as the script would be; only the words are found in the moment.

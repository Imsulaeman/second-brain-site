# Lateral Reading

Lateral reading is the practice of leaving a webpage to investigate the source, claim, or organization through other tabs and other sources rather than trusting what the page says about itself.

It is the opposite of default vertical reading, where a person stays inside the page and judges credibility from internal cues such as design, tone, citations, or polish. On the modern web, those cues are often easy to fake. Lateral reading shifts the question from "does this page look credible?" to "what does the wider web say about the people making this claim?"

The concept matters because it is built for the structure of online information. Credibility is often discovered somewhere else.

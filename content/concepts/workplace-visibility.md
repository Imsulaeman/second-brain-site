# Workplace Visibility

[[entities/Vinh-Giang|Vinh Giang]]'s claim that career progression tracks how much someone contributes out loud in meetings, not just how good their work is. In his framing, visibility is the person who speaks up in team meetings; invisibility is the person who does strong technical work and says nothing, on the assumption that "my work will speak for itself." When promotion decisions get made, it's the visible person who gets picked — not necessarily the more technically capable one.

Giang illustrates this with a recurring, self-deprecating example from his own career: a university acquaintance he calls "Brad," less technically capable but consistently more vocal in meetings, who kept getting promoted ahead of him. Giang's response for years was to double down on "letting his work speak for itself" — which, in his account, only kept him invisible longer. What changed his trajectory wasn't better work; it was using a structure (the [[concepts/para-framework|PARA framework]]) to convert his existing ideas into short, credible spoken contributions.

## The Mechanism

The claim isn't that visibility should outweigh competence — it's that competence without visibility often loses to visibility with competence, because promotion decisions are made by people who can only evaluate what they've actually seen or heard. Silent excellence is invisible by construction. The fix Giang recommends isn't becoming a louder personality; it's structuring the *content* of a contribution (point, action taken, result achieved, ask) so that speaking up doesn't require winging it.

## Not to Be Confused With

This is a different concept from [[concepts/visibility-paradox|the visibility paradox]] elsewhere in the wiki, which is about the fear of being noticed suppressing social belonging. That visibility is about being seen as yourself in relationships; this visibility is about being credited for contributions at work. The word overlaps; the mechanism and domain don't.

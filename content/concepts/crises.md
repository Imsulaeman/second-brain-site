Crises are periodic breakdowns in the circulation of money, goods, and confidence that define market societies.

*Talking to My Daughter About the Economy* links crises to the debt-profit relationship and the failure of recycling. It argues that crises are not external shocks but endogenous to a system in which profit expectations and debt obligations can become misaligned. The book uses both the 1930s and the Greek/Eurozone crisis as examples.

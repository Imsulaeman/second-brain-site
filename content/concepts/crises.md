Crisis in this book is not an external interruption to an otherwise balanced system. It is what happens when the inner mechanisms of capitalism stop cohering.

Varoufakis ties crises to the marriage of [[concepts/debt-and-profit|debt and profit]] and the breakdown of [[concepts/recycling|recycling]]. Firms, banks, households, and states all rely on promises about future payment. Once those promises no longer line up with actual sales, incomes, or confidence, the system seizes. The result is not just recession but social absurdity: goods exist, needs exist, and yet money and coordination fail.

That is why the book treats the 1930s and the Greek crisis as windows into the same logic rather than unrelated disasters.

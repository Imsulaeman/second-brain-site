# AI Hallucinations

AI hallucinations are fabricated or unsupported outputs generated by an AI system that are presented with the style of confident knowledge. The critical property is that hallucination is not random noise. It is a predictable consequence of how these systems work.

## Why They Happen

Generative models are optimized to produce plausible continuations of input — not to verify claims against ground truth. The model does not retrieve from a database of true facts. It statistically assembles tokens that fit the context. When that pattern-assembly produces a confident-sounding sequence that happens to be false, the system has no internal mechanism to notice the gap. The output can be polished, specific, and even include citations — all fabricated.

This means even improving training data does not eliminate hallucination. The generation process itself can combine valid patterns into invalid outputs.

## What Hallucinates

The failure mode appears most sharply with:

- **Specific facts**: dates, statistics, names, prices — anything that is verifiable but not unambiguously encoded in patterns
- **Citations**: authors, papers, cases — the model can generate plausible-sounding references that do not exist
- **Legal and medical specifics**: jurisdictions, dosages, case law — high-stakes domains where confident wrongness causes real harm
- **Detailed procedural steps**: when asked to explain a process, the model can fabricate steps that sound procedurally correct

## Why It Matters More Than "Sometimes Wrong"

The dangerous version of AI hallucination is not the obvious mistake. It is the output confident enough to pass an uncritical reader, specific enough to seem authoritative, and wrong enough to cause downstream harm. Research errors, legal citations, academic fraud — these are downstream failure modes, not edge cases.

## What To Do

The practical response is not to stop using AI but to change how you treat its output:

1. Treat AI output as draft material that requires verification, not as self-authenticating answers
2. Check facts, citations, and specific claims using [[concepts/lateral-reading]] and the [[concepts/sift-method]]
3. Prefer retrieval-augmented systems (RAG) when available — outputs grounded in retrieved documents fail differently than pure generation
4. Prompt more precisely: constraining scope reduces the surface area for confabulation
5. Apply [[concepts/critical-thinking]] to claims that feel too convenient or too complete

Hallucination is not a fixable bug in the classical sense. It is a structural feature of a particular approach to language modeling. Understanding that shifts how you calibrate trust.

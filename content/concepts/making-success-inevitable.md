# Making Success Inevitable

A distinction between two operating modes for project ownership:

**Mode 1:** "My goal is to ship by end of month, so I'm going to get people started ASAP."

**Mode 2:** "My goal is to ship by end of month, so I'm going to list everything that needs to happen, draw up a schedule backwards from the ship date, check the critical path, verify staffing, decide what gets cut if we slip, add honest slop, track progress against the schedule, surface slippage the moment I see it, pull in people if needed…"

Mode 1 makes you a leaky abstraction — someone else has to constantly monitor the project and resolve blockers. Mode 2 means others can stop spending substantial mental energy worrying about whether your project will land.

"Inevitable" is the bar. Not "I'll try hard" or "I'm on it." Inevitable means: given what I know, this outcome is going to happen unless something unexpected breaks it — and if something unexpected does break it, I'll know immediately and escalate.

## Why it's rare

People who can be trusted to make something inevitable are the bottleneck for how many different things a team or organization can do at once. If someone else has to make your project inevitable, you're consuming that scarce resource. If you make your own projects inevitable, you're producing it and directly unlocking team capacity.

The bar is also higher than it looks. Inevitable requires:
- Knowing the full critical path, not just your part of it
- Tracking progress against a concrete plan, not vibes
- Being willing to escalate, cut scope, or ask for help before it's obvious to others that you should

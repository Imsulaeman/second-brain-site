Automation refers to the replacement of human labor by machines. In capitalist economies this creates a recurring contradiction: machines raise productivity but also reduce the number of people earning wages, undermining the consumer demand that capitalism requires.

In *Talking to My Daughter About the Economy*, Varoufakis explores this tension through the metaphor of "haunted machines" (drawing on Frankenstein and The Matrix). The machines are not inherently malevolent, but under current rules they intensify the tendency toward unemployment and crisis. The book argues that the solution is political — changing the distribution of the gains from automation — rather than technological.

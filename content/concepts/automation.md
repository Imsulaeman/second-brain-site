Automation is the use of machines to reduce or replace human labor, but in this book it matters less as a technology story than as a contradiction inside capitalism itself.

Varoufakis's haunted machines are frightening because they expose a structural tension. Firms are rewarded for replacing workers with machines, yet workers' wages are also what sustain demand for the goods firms hope to sell. The more successful automation becomes under existing rules, the more it threatens the income base on which the system depends.

The book therefore rejects the lazy debate between techno-optimism and technophobia. Machines are not the enemy. The question is who benefits from productivity gains, who absorbs the displacement, and whether the surrounding rules turn technology into shared freedom or deeper instability.

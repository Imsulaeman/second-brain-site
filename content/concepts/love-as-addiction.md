# Love as Addiction

Romantic love activates the same neural reward pathways as substances like cocaine and opioids. When a relationship ends, the brain goes through withdrawal — not metaphorically, but neurologically. Brain imaging studies confirm it.

This explains why [[concepts/heartbreak]] is so hard to heal and why ordinary coping mechanisms fail. The heartbroken person isn't grieving a loss in the conventional sense — they are also managing an active addiction and its withdrawal symptoms.

## The Behavioral Consequence

The addiction mechanism produces a specific trap: behaviors that feel like processing the loss are actually feeding the craving.

- Replaying memories → the same as a drug craving satisfied by using
- Stalking the ex on social media → feeding the fix
- Obsessively reconstructing what went wrong → a mental hit, not resolution
- Seeking "closure" through more conversation → another dose

The person believes they are solving a mystery or processing emotions. They are getting high. The distinction matters because addicts know when they're using; heartbroken people usually don't.

## What This Reframes

Recovery from heartbreak requires the same discipline as recovery from addiction: recognizing the craving for what it is, refusing to feed it, and accepting that no amount of "using" will satisfy it — each indulgence deepens the withdrawal rather than shortening it.

Hope for reconciliation functions as the equivalent of keeping drugs in the house. It sustains the addiction by keeping the possibility of a fix alive.

# Logotherapy

Meaning-centered psychotherapy developed by [[entities/Viktor-Frankl|Viktor Frankl]]. The Third Viennese School of Psychotherapy — after Freud's psychoanalysis (pleasure principle) and Adler's individual psychology (will to power). Where those schools look backward to causes, logotherapy looks forward to meaning the patient still has left to fulfill.

## Core Claims

**The will to meaning is the primary human motivation.** Not pleasure, not power, not tension reduction. This is not an abstract claim — it survived empirical test under the most extreme conditions possible: concentration camps. Frankl observed directly that those with a "why" — a child waiting, an unfinished manuscript, a person to return to — survived better. Those who lost their meaning gave up within days.

**Existential frustration is not automatically pathological.** When the will to meaning is blocked, the result is [[concepts/existential-vacuum|existential frustration]] — a pervasive feeling of emptiness. This is uncomfortable, but it is sometimes proof of one's humanness, not a symptom requiring suppression.

**Noögenic neuroses require logotherapy, not psychotherapy.** Some neuroses arise from existential problems — meaninglessness, moral conflict, existential guilt — not from psychological mechanisms. Standard psychotherapy treats the wrong layer. Frankl called these *noögenic* neuroses (arising from the *noös*, the spiritual dimension).

## Three Avenues to Meaning

Logotherapy identifies three ways a person can find meaning:

1. **Creative work or deed** — making something, contributing something, doing something that matters
2. **Experiencing or encountering** — receiving beauty, truth, or love; the experience of another person fully and specifically ("The salvation of man is through love and in love")
3. **Attitude toward unavoidable suffering** — when circumstances cannot be changed, the person's attitude toward them can be. This is the most powerful of the three: even in suffering, meaning is available. See [[concepts/meaning-in-suffering]].

## Noö-Dynamics

Frankl rejected homeostasis as the goal of the psyche. Healthy functioning is not equilibrium — it is *noö-dynamics*: productive tension between what one is and what one ought to become. A person without a challenge to orient toward is not at peace; they are at risk of the existential vacuum. "What man actually needs is not a tensionless state but rather the striving and struggling for a worthwhile goal."

## Techniques

**Paradoxical intention** — the patient is instructed to intend or wish for the thing they fear. This breaks the anticipatory anxiety feedback loop. Effective for phobias, OCD, and sleep-onset anxiety. Requires humor and self-detachment. See [[concepts/paradoxical-intention]].

**Dereflection** — the counter-technique to hyper-reflection. Instead of turning attention toward symptoms or internal states (which amplifies them via *hyper-reflection*), the patient is redirected outward toward something meaningful. Pleasure and orgasm, for example, only disappear when directly pursued — the pleasure principle is, as Frankl put it, "a fun-spoiler."

**The categorical imperative of logotherapy:** "Live as if you were living already for the second time and had acted as wrongly the first time as you are about to act now." This converts abstract responsibility into immediate urgency.

## Pan-Determinism Critique

Logotherapy resists the view that humans are fully conditioned by biology, psychology, or society. This does not deny causality — it asserts that between stimulus and response there is a space, and in that space lies the human capacity to choose one's attitude. Frankl's argument is not theoretical; it is biographical. He saw it survive the worst conditions possible.

See also [[concepts/last-freedom]].

# Social Brain Hypothesis

Also called the **Machiavellian Intelligence Hypothesis**. Human intelligence evolved primarily to navigate social competition — not to solve ecological challenges like hunting, tool use, or fire.

The distinction matters because:
- **Ecological explanations** imply intelligence grew to help us *cooperate* against the environment. Flattering. Win-win narrative.
- **Social explanations** imply intelligence grew to help us *outcompete other humans*. Unflattering. Zero-sum narrative.

Most researchers, for understandable reasons, spend more time looking under the ecological streetlamp. But the evidence points toward social competition.

## The evidence

1. **Brain size across primate species** correlates with social group size, not ecological complexity. Species that live in larger, more politically complex groups have bigger relative brain sizes.
2. **The cognitive demands of social life** — tracking alliances, detecting cheaters, modeling others' beliefs, managing reputation — are higher than those of ecological challenges like foraging.
3. **The pace of brain growth** in hominid evolution was faster than ecological changes alone can explain.

## What this implies

Our deepest cognitive machinery — theory of mind, language, deception detection, coalition tracking — evolved not for science or engineering but for political maneuvering in small groups. We're running savanna-politics software on industrial-civilization hardware.

This is the deep origin of [[concepts/hidden-motives]]: if intelligence evolved for social competition, then much of what we think and do is oriented toward competitive social goals, even when we think we're doing something else.

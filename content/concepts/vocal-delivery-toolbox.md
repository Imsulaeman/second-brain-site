# Vocal Delivery Toolbox

[[entities/Julian-Treasure|Julian Treasure]]'s frame for the voice as an instrument with adjustable controls. Most people have never opened the toolbox — they speak from habit, not choice.

What you say matters; how you say it determines whether anyone stays to hear it.

## Register

Where the voice physically sits:

- **Nose** — thin, higher placement
- **Throat** — where most people speak from by default
- **Chest** — lower, weighted; carries authority

Treasure notes that voters prefer politicians with lower voices — we associate depth with power and authority. Register is a lever for perceived weight, not just audibility.

## Timbre

How the voice *feels* to the listener — rich, smooth, warm ("like hot chocolate" in Treasure's analogy). If your natural timbre isn't warm, it's trainable: breathing, posture, and exercises (or a voice coach) can reshape it.

## Prosody

The sing-song layer that imparts meaning through rhythm and intonation. See [[concepts/prosody]] for monotone and uptalk failure modes.

## Pace

Speed carries arousal. Fast for excitement; slow for emphasis. At the end of a slowed passage, **silence** is a legitimate tool — no need to fill pauses with "um" and "ah."

## Pitch

Independent of pace, pitch shifts meaning. The same words at different pitches can imply different questions, emotions, or emphasis.

## Volume

Loud projects excitement; quiet can command attention more effectively than shouting. The failure mode is **[[concepts/sodcasting|sodcasting]]** — broadcasting at full volume all the time, carelessly imposing sound on everyone nearby.

## When to Use the Toolbox

Treasure reserves full attention to these controls for high-stakes speech: TED talks, marriage proposals, raise requests, wedding toasts. The toolbox pairs with [[concepts/vocal-warmup|vocal warmup]] — no engine runs well cold.

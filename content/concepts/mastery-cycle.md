# Mastery Cycle

[[entities/Vinh-Giang|Vinh Giang]]'s three-stage loop — try and apply, reflect and review, plan and improve — for developing a skill through repeated cycles rather than through accumulated knowledge. Its purpose is narrow and specific: to be the exit ramp from the [[concepts/dunning-kruger-learning-curve|Valley of Despair]]. Giang's diagnosis of why people get stuck there is that they keep raising their knowledge without ever converting it into action — "applied knowledge is power," not knowledge by itself.

## The Three Stages

1. **Try and apply** — use what you just learned immediately, before consuming more input. This has to come first, because a cycle that starts with more learning just adds another layer to the pile that never gets used.
2. **Reflect and review** — after applying, examine what happened. What worked, what didn't, what was surprising.
3. **Plan and improve** — turn the reflection into a specific adjustment for the next attempt.

The loop then repeats from stage one with the adjustment in place.

## Worked Example

Giang demonstrates the cycle live using the [[concepts/three-two-one-framework|3-2-1 framework]] on the topic of travel, across four passes:

1. He tries "the one thing about travel" but names three things (culture, new ways of thinking, food) — a mislabeling. Reflection catches the mismatch; the plan is to actually stick to one thing.
2. He redoes "the one thing" correctly ("it opens your mind") and reflects that he'd gravitated toward the easiest option first, not because it was best but because it required the least effort.
3. He tries "two types" (travel for pleasure vs. travel for business) and reflects that it felt good to stretch into the harder format.
4. He tries "three steps" (budget, choose travel companions, go) and reflects that this format gave him the most to say — discovering, in the process, that a numbered-steps answer is the same shape as a listicle-style piece of content, which he then improvises live as a mock YouTube video.

Each pass fixes exactly one specific, named flaw from the previous pass rather than a vague "get better" — the reflection stage is doing real diagnostic work, not just producing a feeling of having reviewed.

## Relation to Deliberate Practice and Reflection & Review

The Mastery Cycle is a domain-specific instance of a pattern the wiki already tracks under two other names. [[concepts/deliberate-practice|Deliberate practice]] names the same attempt → feedback → adjustment loop as the general engine of skill acquisition, with an emphasis on working just beyond current ability. [[concepts/reflection-and-review|Reflection and review]] names the periodic-check mechanism that keeps a habit or skill from ossifying once it becomes automatic. Giang's contribution isn't a new mechanism — it's a memorable, three-word-per-stage packaging of the same loop, aimed specifically at spoken communication, and explicitly framed as the antidote to a failure mode (learning without applying) that the other two pages don't name as directly.

A third, non-learning-specific relative: [[concepts/kaizen|kaizen]]'s Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle is the identical shape applied to manufacturing and process improvement rather than personal skill. Try-and-apply maps to Do, reflect-and-review maps to Check, plan-and-improve maps to Act and the next Plan. The convergent structure across trading floors (deliberate practice), personal habits (reflection and review), factories (kaizen), and now spoken communication suggests attempt → feedback → adjustment is closer to a general-purpose learning primitive than a domain-specific technique.

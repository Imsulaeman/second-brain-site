Stories and myths are not decorations around economics in this book. They are part of how economic systems justify themselves and part of how people can learn to see through them.

Varoufakis builds the whole argument through narrative: Aboriginal dispossession, Faust, Frankenstein, the POW camp, *The Matrix*. That is not a simplification gimmick. It reflects his deeper claim that capitalism already lives inside stories of merit, progress, neutrality, and necessity. If those stories are false or selective, better analysis requires better stories.

This makes narrative itself a field of struggle. A myth can hide [[concepts/economic-power|economic power]], but it can also expose it once retold from a different angle.

# Idealization

Idealization is the cognitive distortion of remembering only the best of a person or relationship while minimizing or forgetting the bad. It is an automatic response to loss — the mind cycles through the greatest hits, making the loss feel more painful and the person seem more irreplaceable than they were.

In [[concepts/heartbreak]] it is one of the primary mechanisms that prolongs suffering. You're not grieving the person as they actually were. You're grieving a curated highlight reel.

## The Practical Counter

Guy Winch's method: build an exhaustive written list of every way the person was wrong for you — bad qualities, pet peeves, specific failures, difficult moments. Keep it accessible. Use it actively when idealization starts.

This is not bitterness. It is cognitive rebalancing. The mind will default to the smile, the good feeling, the romantic moment on the mountain. The list provides the counterweight: the frown, the argument coming down the mountain, the two days of silence after.

The goal is an accurate picture of the person, not a negative one. Idealization makes them seem better than they were; the list corrects the distortion.

## Why Writing Matters

The correction has to be explicit and stored externally because the mind won't generate it reliably under the pressure of craving. "Phone, please" — the instruction to pull out the list — works precisely because the list exists and can be consulted when the emotional state would otherwise override the reasoning.

# Talk as a Journey

Framing a talk as a journey changes how you prepare and how the audience experiences it. A journey has a starting point, a destination, and a sequence of steps. So does a good talk.

The metaphor is most useful for unscripted preparation. Instead of memorizing sentences, you map the steps: what does the audience know at the start, what does each section add, what do they know at the end? Each step gets a label — a short phrase that describes what happens there and how to get from one to the next.

This produces a navigational skeleton rather than a script. The speaker moves through the journey by hitting each waypoint; the words are found in the moment. As long as the steps are clear and the [[concepts/throughline]] is solid, the talk holds together even without verbatim recall.

## Why the Metaphor Works

A journey implies forward motion. Every step has a purpose: it moves the audience from where they were to where they're going. This filters out content that doesn't move anything — interesting material that doesn't serve the destination.

It also implies that the audience is a companion, not an audience. The speaker's job is to guide them, not perform at them.

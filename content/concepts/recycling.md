Recycling is Varoufakis's name for the circulation that keeps an economy alive: wages returning as spending, revenues returning as salaries and investment, and credit sustaining the flow of payment through time.

The ecological metaphor matters. A modern economy is not healthy merely because things are being produced. It is healthy only if purchasing power keeps moving. Once recycling fails, absurdities appear: food is destroyed while people go hungry, factories sit idle while needs remain unmet, and debt claims survive even as the social world around them breaks.

This is one of the book's sharpest teaching devices because it makes [[concepts/crises|crisis]] visible as metabolic failure, not just as a bad quarterly result.

Recycling refers to the continuous circulation of purchasing power through the economy — wages spent in shops, revenues paid as salaries, loans creating new spending.

When this circulation breaks down (as in the Great Depression or the Greek crisis), the result is not just lower output but the destruction of usable goods while people go hungry. The book uses this ecological metaphor to explain why crises are not mere "downturns" but failures of the system's basic metabolism.

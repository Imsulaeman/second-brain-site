# Keep Your Identity Small

When people's sense of self is attached to a position, they stop evaluating that position on its merits. They start defending themselves instead of thinking. The argument becomes useless — not because the topic is hard, but because the discussion has been taken over by identity protection.

This is what "degenerating into a religious war" actually means: it means the discussion has started to be driven mostly by people's identities rather than by logic and evidence.

## Why politics and religion

These topics have three features that invite identity attachment:
1. No expertise threshold — anyone can have a strong opinion without study
2. Strong social signals — your position on these topics marks your tribe
3. No back pressure — there's rarely a clean way to be shown wrong

But the same topics can be discussable or not depending on who's in the room. Discussing which Bronze Age civilization was strongest is historical analysis. Discussing which modern country is best starts wars — not because the topic changed, but because people's identities are now involved. **It's the participants, not the topic, that determine whether identity gets engaged.**

## The cost

"The more labels you have for yourself, the dumber they make you."

Each label is a filter on what you can consider. If you're a "libertarian," certain conclusions are unavailable to you before you've read the evidence. If you're a "rationalist," other conclusions are unavailable. Every identity commitment is a pre-commitment that overrides a future evaluation.

## The exception: scientist as placeholder

"Scientist" may be a net-win identity — if treated as a methodological placeholder (committed to following evidence) rather than a substantive position. But only if the commitment is genuinely to process rather than to a particular set of conclusions.

## Connection to disagreement levels

Identity engagement explains why people get stuck at DH0-DH2 in certain discussions. When your identity is attached to a position, you can't evaluate counterarguments — you can only defend against them.

# Disagreement Hierarchy

A way to classify the form of disagreement, from weakest to strongest. Higher levels require more work and are rarer, forming a kind of pyramid.

## Levels

- **DH0 Name-calling**: "u r a fag" or pretentious equivalent ("self-important dilettante"). Zero weight.
- **DH1 Ad Hominem**: Attack the person (e.g., "Of course he would say that. He's a senator."). May be relevant but does not refute the argument.
- **DH2 Responding to Tone**: Criticize how it was said rather than what. Tone is secondary and hard to judge objectively.
- **DH3 Contradiction**: State the opposing case with little or no evidence.
- **DH4 Counterargument**: Contradiction plus reasoning/evidence. Often aimed at a slightly different point.
- **DH5 Refutation**: Quote a mistaken passage ("smoking gun") and explain why it is mistaken.
- **DH6 Refuting the Central Point**: Refute the main point (or something it depends on), explicitly committing to what that point is.

## Properties and Value

DH levels describe *form*, not truth — a DH6 can be wrong. They set an upper bound: DH2 or lower is always unconvincing.

Uses:
- Critical reading tool to see through forceful but empty arguments.
- Helps writers recognize and move up from low levels.
- Real arguments don't need meanness; higher levels correlate with less meanness and better outcomes.

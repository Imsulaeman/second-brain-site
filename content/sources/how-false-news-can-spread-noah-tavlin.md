# How false news can spread - Noah Tavlin

This short explainer shows how falsehoods can gain the appearance of credibility through repetition. Its core focus is [[concepts/circular-reporting|circular reporting]]: one outlet publishes misinformation, another repeats it, and the repetition is then mistaken for independent verification.

## Repetition Is Not Confirmation

The page's best lesson is that the modern speed of media circulation makes falsehoods look corroborated long before they have actually been checked. Once several outlets appear to be saying the same thing, readers and later writers can start treating the claim as established.

## How Falsehoods Gain Traction

The source gives a few routes: bad science amplified by attention, satire mistaken for reporting, and user-generated reference pages feeding unverified claims back into more formal publication channels. In each case the common pattern is the same: people substitute citation chains for source scrutiny.

## What To Do Instead

The source's practical advice is modest but strong: avoid sensationalist media, search for criticism of suspicious claims, and trace information back to its original source. In other words, break the loop before repetition hardens into apparent truth.

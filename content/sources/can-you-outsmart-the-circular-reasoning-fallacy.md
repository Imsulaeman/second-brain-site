# Can You Outsmart the Circular Reasoning Fallacy?

**Author:** [[entities/elizabeth-cox|Elizabeth Cox]]
**Type:** Educational reasoning essay

---

## Core Premise

The essay uses the Virginia Military Institute case to explain **circular reasoning** or **begging the question**: an argument that assumes what it is supposed to prove.

---

## Historical / Legal Example: VMI

Virginia tried to defend VMI's male-only admissions policy by treating single-sex education as both the means and the objective. The essay shows why this fails:

- exclusion was defended by pointing back to the institution's existing exclusion
- tradition was treated as if it were justification
- the real public mission, producing citizen-soldiers and leaders, did not inherently require excluding women

Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg's reasoning is presented as a demand for independent justification rather than repetition of the status quo.

---

## Main Lesson

Circular reasoning often hides inside traditions, institutions, and familiar categories. The antidote is to ask what independent reason actually supports the conclusion.

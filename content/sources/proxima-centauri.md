# Proxima Centauri

**Author:** Anonymous personal essay
**Type:** Reflective essay

---

## Core Premise

The essay is about what it feels like to discover the global scoreboard early while being born far outside the systems that feed it. The central distinction is between **delta** (how far you have moved from your origin) and **level** (where you actually stand now in absolute terms). Both matter, but confusing one for the other distorts ambition.

---

## Local Maxima vs. the Real Game

![[proxima-local-maxima.png]]

The narrator frames early achievement as winning inside a local maximum while already knowing there is a much taller global peak elsewhere. That creates a split experience:

- pride, because the climb from origin was real
- resentment, because the local game was never the real benchmark
- instability, because the benchmark changes as soon as you enter a larger world

This is a clean articulation of the pain of **seeing the true game too early** without yet having the map, language, or network to enter it.

---

## Delta vs. Level

![[proxima-delta-slope.png]]

![[proxima-delta-convergence.png]]

The essay's most useful distinction is [[concepts/delta-vs-level]]:

- **Delta** asks how far you have moved from where you started
- **Level** asks where you stand right now against the actual field

Delta is morally and psychologically important because starting points are unequal. But level is what the world prices. The essay rejects both cheap exits:

- **resentment** — insisting your slope should count more than their position
- **collapse** — deciding the larger game is proof that your earlier effort meant nothing

The proposed stance is harder: honor the cost of the climb without asking origin to become a permanent excuse.

---

## Gamma and Convexity

![[proxima-crossover.png]]

The second useful frame is the essay's use of **gamma** as delayed convexity. Some people inherit compounding structures early: language, elite schools, networks, money, legibility. Others initially move linearly and only later build a steep slope.

The key insight is that effort does not always look rewarding at first because some work is really the purchase of a better curve. What looks like falling behind can be the dip before convex compounding appears.

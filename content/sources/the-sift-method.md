# Library Guides Evaluating Resources and Misinformation The SIFT Method

This guide presents Mike Caulfield's [[concepts/sift-method|SIFT method]] as a compact routine for evaluating online claims before accepting or sharing them. Its strength is not theoretical depth. It is practical sequencing.

## Four Moves

The method is: Stop, Investigate the Source, Find Better Coverage, and Trace claims, quotes, and media to the original context. Taken together, these moves turn vague caution into a repeatable workflow.

## Why It Works

SIFT is built for the speed and deception of networked information. It assumes that emotional triggers, branded credibility, and reposted fragments are part of the normal web environment. Instead of asking readers to become omniscient, it gives them a disciplined order of operations.

## Relation To Lateral Reading

The guide is strongest when paired with [[concepts/lateral-reading|lateral reading]]. SIFT gives the checklist. Lateral reading gives the movement pattern. Together they push the reader away from trusting self-presentation and toward external verification.

Worth coming back to: SIFT matters because it compresses good information hygiene into four memorable actions that can be reused under time pressure.

# Can You Outsmart the Argument from Ignorance Fallacy?

**Author:** [[entities/elizabeth-cox|Elizabeth Cox]]
**Type:** Educational reasoning essay

---

## Core Premise

The essay uses McCarthyism and the accusation of [[entities/dorothy-kenyon|Dorothy Kenyon]] to explain the **argument from ignorance**: treating a claim as true because it has not been proven false.

---

## Historical Example: McCarthyism

Joseph McCarthy's accusations gained force not from strong evidence but from fear and reversal of burden of proof. The reasoning error was:

- accuse first
- demand disproof from the accused
- treat lack of immediate disproof as political credibility

The essay argues that this structure is dangerous because it turns accusation into an unfalsifiable trap, especially when the alleged activity is secret or hard to disprove.

---

## Main Lesson

The essential principle is that **the claimant must supply the evidence**. A lack of disproof is not proof. This matters in politics, law, science, and public discourse because otherwise rumor and fear can destroy people before facts are established.

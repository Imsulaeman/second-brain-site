# Poor Charlie's Almanack

**Author:** [[entities/charles-munger]] (edited by Peter D. Kaufman)
**Published:** 2005 (expanded edition)
**Format:** Collection of talks, speeches, and biographical material

---

## Overview

A compilation of Munger's most important talks and ideas, modeled on Ben Franklin's *Poor Richard's Almanac*. The book covers biography, Munger's approach to life and investing, "Mungerisms" from Berkshire annual meetings, and eleven major talks — culminating in his magnum opus on the psychology of human misjudgment.

---

## Central Thesis

The key to good judgment is building a **[[concepts/mental-models-latticework|latticework of mental models]]** from every major discipline — math, physics, biology, psychology, economics, engineering, history — and using them routinely as a checklist. Single-discipline thinking ([[concepts/man-with-a-hammer-syndrome|man-with-a-hammer syndrome]]) produces reliable errors. Multidisciplinary fluency, combined with awareness of psychological misjudgment, produces disproportionately good outcomes.

## Major Ideas

### Thinking Tools
- **[[concepts/mental-models-latticework]]** — ~100 models from all disciplines, organized into a usable lattice
- **[[concepts/inversion]]** — Jacobi's method: solve problems backward by identifying what to avoid
- **[[concepts/man-with-a-hammer-syndrome]]** — single-tool thinking warps every problem into one shape
- **[[concepts/planck-vs-chauffeur-knowledge]]** — real understanding vs. learned prattle; know the difference
- **[[concepts/circle-of-competence]]** — know your limits; stay inside them or expand them deliberately
- **[[concepts/iron-prescription]]** — force yourself to consider disconfirming evidence, especially for ideas you love

### Psychology of Human Misjudgment (25 Tendencies)
Munger's **[[concepts/psychology-of-human-misjudgment]]** catalogs 25 standard causes of cognitive error, each with real-world examples and antidotes. The critical meta-insight is the **[[concepts/lollapalooza-effect]]**: when multiple tendencies act in confluence, the result is extreme and often catastrophic. Key tendencies include:

1. **[[concepts/incentive-superpower]]** — reward/punishment as the #1 behavior driver
2. Liking/Loving and Disliking/Hating — distort facts to serve affection or hatred
3. Doubt-Avoidance + Inconsistency-Avoidance — rush to conclusions, then refuse to change them
4. Reciprocation — subconscious debt from favors; exploited by compliance practitioners
5. Social Proof — follow the crowd, especially under stress
6. Deprival-Superreaction — loss aversion on steroids; drives gambling compulsion and sunk-cost escalation
7. Contrast-Misreaction — small incremental changes mask trends toward disaster
8. Excessive Self-Regard — overestimate yourself, your possessions, your conclusions
9. Availability-Misweighing — overweight vivid/recent data; antidote is checklists
10. Authority-Misinfluence — follow leaders too far, even into obvious error

### Economics & Business
- **[[concepts/scale-effects]]** — advantages (specialization, volume) and disadvantages (bureaucracy, incentive decay) of scale
- **[[concepts/gaming-of-systems]]** — all human systems get gamed; anti-gaming features are essential design
- **[[concepts/febezzlement]]** — Galbraith's "bezzle" generalized: unreported wealth destruction that feels real until revealed
- Virtue and vice effects — cash registers, double-entry bookkeeping, and reliability as economic forces
- Academic economics critique (9 defects): psychological ignorance, physics envy, too little synthesis, neglect of [[concepts/second-order-thinking|second-order effects]]

### Investing
- **[[concepts/sit-on-your-ass-investing]]** — pari-mutuel analogy; patience and concentrated bets over hyperactive diversification
- Moats, competitive destruction, the "surfing" model for riding new technology waves

### Life Wisdom (USC Commencement)
- **[[concepts/seamless-web-of-trust]]** — deserved trust as the highest form of organization
- Lifetime learning as moral duty — Buffett reads half his waking hours
- **[[concepts/stoic-resilience]]** — Epictetus: every mischance is an opportunity to behave well
- **[[concepts/grannys-rule]]** — do the unpleasant task first, then reward yourself
- **[[concepts/persian-messenger-syndrome]]** — don't kill the bearer of bad news; Berkshire's antidote: "Always tell us the bad news promptly"
- Avoid intense ideology, self-serving bias, envy, and self-pity
- Non-egalitarianism (Wooden model): maximize playing time for your best players

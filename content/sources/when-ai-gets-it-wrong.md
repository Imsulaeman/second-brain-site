# When AI Gets It Wrong Addressing AI Hallucinations and Bias

This guide is a practical warning that generative AI failure is not one problem but at least two: [[concepts/ai-hallucinations|hallucinations]] and [[concepts/ai-bias|bias]]. It argues that both are structural consequences of how these models are trained and how they generate outputs, not mere temporary bugs.

## Two Failure Modes

On one side, generative systems can fabricate facts, cases, citations, and explanations that sound authoritative. On the other side, they can reproduce or amplify social distortions found in training data, especially around race, gender, and representation. The page is strongest when it shows that both problems can cause real-world harm far beyond embarrassment.

## Why The Problems Persist

The guide emphasizes that these systems are optimized for plausible continuation, not for truth-verification. Even if the training corpus improves, generation itself can still combine patterns into false outputs. Likewise, biased source material and biased social patterns can be reproduced at scale.

## What To Do About It

Its practical response is straightforward: evaluate outputs critically, diversify verification sources, prefer retrieval-grounded systems when possible, prompt more clearly, and treat AI outputs as draft material requiring human judgment rather than as self-authenticating answers.

Worth coming back to: this source matters because it moves the conversation past "AI sometimes makes mistakes" into a clearer picture of the distinct mechanisms by which it fails.

# How to Introduce Yourself — and Get Hired

[[entities/Rebecca-Okamoto|Rebecca Okamoto]]'s TEDx talk on the four words that make people ramble or freeze — "Tell me about yourself" — and how a [[concepts/twenty-word-introduction|20-word introduction]] built around the listener can open doors that qualifications alone cannot.

## The Wake-Up Call

Okamoto blew a supply-chain instructor interview despite 20 years at Procter & Gamble. She prepared an elevator pitch listing every accomplishment. When asked "tell me about yourself," she explained everything she could do.

The interviewer stopped her: it would have been more effective to explain what she could do *for them*. Five-minute interview. Rejected on the spot.

Six months later, the same person answered "What do you do?" in one sentence — *"I'm a communications consultant and I help people with something to say but struggle to say it"* — and heard "Tell me more." Hired 15 minutes later for entrepreneur workshops, with fewer qualifications than the failed interview.

The only variable that changed: the introduction.

## Two Secrets

1. **Think sound bite** — multitasking audiences have goldfish-level attention spans. A 20-word intro is designed to grab attention and trigger "Tell me more," not to summarize your résumé.

2. **Don't talk about yourself** — explain what you can do for *them*. Okamoto calls this the [[concepts/about-you-introduction|about-you vs. about-me]] distinction:
   - About-me: *"I'm an award-winning, best-selling author."*
   - About-you: *"I help new authors get published faster."*

## Five Formulas

All target 20 words or less. Full templates: [[concepts/twenty-word-introduction]].

| Framework | When to use | Formula shape |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Benefit** | Career-focused, straightforward | I help [audience] achieve [benefit they desire] |
| **Breakthrough** | Differentiation via contrast | Same + *without* [negative consequence] |
| **Passion** | Mission-driven, pivoting, student | I'm passionate about [X] to achieve [audience value] |
| **Strength** | Known capability | I'm known for [strength] to achieve [audience value] |
| **Mission** | Purpose-led | I'm on a mission to [ABC] to achieve [audience value] |

A benefit answers the listener's implicit question: *How will my life be different?* The word **without** in the breakthrough formula explains what makes you unique.

Different audiences value different things — expect to use more than one version.

## The Imposter Reframe

Okamoto closes with a challenge: when doors close and the inner critic says you're not good enough, smart enough, or that you're an imposter — consider that the problem may not be you. It may be the introduction. The gap between being seen and being overlooked might be 20 words, not missing qualifications.

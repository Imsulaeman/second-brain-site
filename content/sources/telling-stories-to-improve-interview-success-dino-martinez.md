# Telling Stories to Improve Interview Success

[[entities/Dino-Martinez|Dino Martinez]]'s TEDx talk: once your resume gets you the interview, become a **storyteller** — reach into your rolodex of experiences and show how they shaped who you are in front of the interviewer.

## Core Argument

They like you on paper. The interview is time to breathe life into the resume. More employers use **behavioral-based** formats ("tell me about a time when…") instead of hypotheticals. Stories — not adjectives — answer those prompts.

## Research Stack

1. Company, agency, or school
2. Interviewers (ask who's in the room; check LinkedIn)
3. **Yourself** — what do you bring that four equally qualified peers don't? Generic answers ("dedicated, hardworking") collapse under comparison

## Tell Me About Yourself

Use [[concepts/present-past-future-introduction|present → past → future]] in 2–3 minutes, tied to the position. Don't be five minutes in and still in fifth grade.

## Behavioral Answers

[[concepts/star-method|STAR]] (Situation, Task, Action, Results) or CAR (Context, Action, Results). The interviewer often doesn't care about the mistake itself — they care what you did, learned, and how it shaped your development. End with insight, not just "everything was fine."

## Beyond Behavioral

- **Hypotheticals:** answer with a similar real story (lost flash drive → found someone's drive)
- **Technical:** illustrate knowledge through client/project stories (Java vs C++ via two clients)
- **Nerves:** channel anxiety into eagerness — you're prepared because you researched yourself

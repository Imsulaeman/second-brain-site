# Can You Outsmart the Slippery Slope Fallacy?

**Author:** [[entities/elizabeth-cox|Elizabeth Cox]]
**Type:** Educational reasoning essay

---

## Core Premise

The essay uses Eisenhower's domino theory about Vietnam to explain the **slippery slope fallacy**: treating one possible chain of consequences as if it were inevitable.

---

## Historical Example: Domino Theory

The concern was that communist control of Vietnam would trigger cascading regional collapse. The essay's critique is not that consequences were impossible, but that the chain was treated with too much certainty.

It emphasizes:

- each extra step lowers confidence in the whole chain
- politics behaves more like a web than a clean line of dominoes
- fear makes extreme outcomes feel more probable than they are

---

## Main Lesson

Not every warning about consequences is fallacious. The error appears when uncertainty, branching possibilities, and probability are collapsed into a single deterministic story.

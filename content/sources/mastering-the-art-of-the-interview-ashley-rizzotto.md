# Mastering the Art of the Interview

[[entities/Ashley-Rizzotto|Ashley Rizzotto]]'s TEDx talk reframes the "perfect interview" — not as saying what employers want to hear, but as preparing before you arrive so you can advocate for what you actually need.

## The Wrong Interview

Rizzotto nailed her first big interview: power outfit, rehearsed answers, heads nodding, offer next day. Ten months later she burned out — micromanaged, overwhelmed, no work-home boundary. The job she loved (using strengths) was a small fraction of her responsibilities.

In the interview she'd told them what they wanted to hear: great at criticism, good at logistics and planning — when she honestly hated planning. The excitement of the first offer blinded her. The *idea* of the job mattered more than advocating for what she needed.

## Three Strategies

1. **[[concepts/interview-non-negotiables|Know your non-negotiables]]** — tune into activities and surroundings that bring joy; well-being inside and outside work
2. **[[concepts/career-strengths-identification|Identify your strengths]]** — compliments that stuck, how your best friend would describe you; separate idealized from actual strengths
3. **[[concepts/alignment-through-research|Find alignment through research]]** — match internal needs to external opportunities

## Alignment in Practice

When Rizzotto interviewed for her current career-advisor role, she advocated for independence, creativity, and a coworker "family" culture — all non-negotiables. When asked about criticism, she answered honestly. When asked about logistics, she acknowledged skill but redirected to strengths.

The offer used her own words back: *"Ashley, we already see you as part of our career family."* That mirroring signaled [[concepts/alignment-through-research|alignment]].

## When Research Reveals Gaps

Draw dotted lines between your non-negotiable list and the job posting. If something's missing: (1) advocate in the interview, or (2) step back — this may not be the job for you. Don't settle.

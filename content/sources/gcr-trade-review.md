# GCR Trade Review

A visual retrospective PDF by [[entities/GCR]] (@GiganticRebirth) presenting four annotated trades from the early-2021 crypto bull market. Each entry is a tweet (entry/exit thesis), a position screenshot showing notional size and PnL, and an annotated chart marking the key events. The document functions as a compact teaching tool: GCR shows not just what he traded but why each price level made structural sense.

## Trade 1: CREAM — DeFi Exploit Panic (Feb 13)

Tweet: *"Long every panic sell from defi exploits, because the market literally never cares beyond a couple of hours."*

A DeFi protocol exploit crashed CREAM ~40% — which GCR identifies as the "standard altcoin crash distance." He longed at 176. The annotated chart marks: exploit revealed → 40% crash → Long @ 176 → Tweet posted @ 239, "40% pump likely a good mechanical exit point." PnL: ~$135k on ~$547k notional.

The thesis is precise: DeFi exploits represent real protocol damage, but the asset's market price overshoots because most participants cannot distinguish temporary disruption from permanent impairment. "The market never cares beyond a couple of hours" is a claim about the half-life of fear in liquid markets, not a dismissal of the exploit itself.

## Trade 2: DOGE — Retail Sentiment Signal (Feb 14)

Tweet (reply): *"Although some of the most sagacious young investors I know believe we are still quite early."*

Posted alongside a screenshot of the "How $DOGE can reach $1" YouTube video — a meme of retail conviction. The annotated DOGE chart shows the tweet landing near the early part of the massive DOGE pump that followed in May 2021. This is a sentiment marker, not a direct trade entry. The implication: the cohort GCR was describing (young investors waiting for DOGE to reach $1) represented the retail accumulation phase — the setup that preceded the final liquidity cascade.

## Trade 3: BTC — Liquidation Cascade (Feb 24)

Tweet: *"Liquidations are a forced transfer of wealth from impoverished traders who need leverage — to wealthy spot buyers. Did you capitalize?"*

Annotated chart: OI + funding at unsustainable levels, spot buyers exhausted → 20% crash (standard BTC crash distance) → Long @ 45,000 → tweet posted @ 51,193.

The position snapshot shows BTC-PERP, ETH-PERP, SUSHI-PERP, and a fourth position — aggregate net PnL of ~$2.1M at tweet time. A portfolio-level trade, not a single-name play.

The mechanism: when OI and funding rates show leveraged longs overextended, the market is loaded with forced sellers-in-waiting. Once spot buyers are exhausted, any sell pressure cascades into mass liquidation. The crash is structural clearing, not fundamental repricing. Spot buyers with dry powder enter after the clearing.

## Trade 4: SUSHI — Meme Resistance Exit (Mar 1)

Tweet: *"Woke: Made 4 million dollars on $SUSHI today [shaved most of my position at $20 meme resistance]. Will look to add back if I see a decisive S/R flip."*

Long @ ~$12, exit at $20. Chart annotation: "Exit at round number $20 meme resistance (ended up being the ATH)." PnL: ~$3.9M on 158k SUSHI.

Two mechanisms named explicitly: **meme resistance** ($20 as a psychologically resonant round number that concentrates sell orders) and the **S/R flip** as re-entry trigger — if $20 breaks and holds above $20, resistance becomes support and re-entry is structurally justified.

## What the Document Teaches

Across four trades, GCR encodes a consistent framework: identify a structural reason for mispricing (fear from an exploit, OI/funding overextension, leveraged liquidation cascade, retail catalyst distribution), wait for a standard crash distance, enter at exhaustion, exit at a psychologically meaningful level. None of the entries rely on predicting news or fundamental analysis. All rely on reading market structure and crowd behavior.

The document also implicitly teaches exit discipline: GCR exits at mechanical points (40% pump, $20 meme resistance, tweet at the entry) rather than holding for maximum gain.

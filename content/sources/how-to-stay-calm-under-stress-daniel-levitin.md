# How to Stay Calm When You Know You'll Be Stressed

[[entities/Daniel-Levitin|Daniel Levitin]]'s TED talk on why you are not at your best under stress — and why the fix is not willpower in the moment but [[concepts/premortem|systems and pre-mortem thinking]] installed before cortisol clouds your judgment.

## The Montreal Story

Locked out at minus 40°C, Levitin broke his basement window to get in. Next morning, cortisol-flooded and sleep-deprived, he forgot his passport, missed his seat, and spent eight hours next to the lavatory. The cascade started with one stressed decision (breaking glass instead of waiting for a locksmith) and compounded because he could not tell his thinking was impaired — cloudy thinking hides from itself.

## Stress and the Brain

Under threat, the brain releases **cortisol**, which raises heart rate, modulates adrenaline, and clouds logical thinking. Evolutionarily useful against predators — you don't need digestion or immune function when running from a lion. Unfortunately, rational thinking is among the systems that degrade.

[[entities/Daniel-Kahneman|Danny Kahneman]]'s work confirms: under stress, System 2 goes offline. The response is not "try harder in the moment" but **prepare when calm**.

## Prospective Hindsight / Pre-Mortem

Kahneman told Levitin about **prospective hindsight** — a technique from psychologist [[entities/Gary-Klein|Gary Klein]], also called the **pre-mortem**:

- **Postmortem:** after disaster, experts figure out what went wrong
- **Pre-mortem:** before acting, imagine what could go wrong, then prevent or minimize damage

Full treatment: [[concepts/premortem]].

## Practical Systems

### Designated places

The [[concepts/hippocampus|hippocampus]] tracks locations of important things — wells, fruit trees, friendly tribes. Excellent for static objects; poor for things that move (keys, glasses, passports). Fix: designate one spot per essential item and be scrupulous about returning it.

### Travel backup

Photograph credit cards, driver's license, and passport; email to yourself so copies live in the cloud. Speeds replacement if lost or stolen.

### Stress-proof access

Levitin's post-trip fix: combination lock box by the door with a spare key inside — prevents repeat lockouts without breaking windows.

## Medical Decisions Under Stress

Medical choices may be the highest-stakes stress decisions. Levitin uses statin prescription as a proxy for any decision needing rational fact assessment.

Ask for the **[[concepts/number-needed-to-treat|number needed to treat]] (NNT)** — how many people must take a treatment before one person benefits. For the most widely prescribed statin: **300**. Then ask about side effects (5% incidence) → 15 harmed per 300 treated. You are **15 times more likely to be harmed than helped**.

Levitin is not prescribing whether to take statins — he is arguing for **informed consent**: you have the right to this conversation. Pre-mortem the questions while calm so you don't manufacture the reasoning chain on the spot.

Also pre-decide quality-of-life tradeoffs: shorter pain-free life vs. longer life with end-stage suffering. Discuss with family now; you may change your mind later, but you'll have practiced the thinking.

Prostate surgery example: NNT = 49; side effects in 50% of patients.
